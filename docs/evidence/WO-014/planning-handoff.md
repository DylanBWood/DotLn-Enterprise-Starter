## Questions to retain

1. Revisit concepts already applied somewhere, as well as untouched concepts.
   Try several contexts and record correspondences, expected differences,
   assumptions and counterexamples. Domain blindness is a reason to keep this
   inquiry open. Bounded cleanup and antifragility are examples supplied by the
   operator; stronger learning claims need observed improvement, not just recovery.
2. Use system traps and anti-patterns as diagnostic vocabulary at several levels.
   Retain symptom, suspected mechanism, intervention and disconfirming evidence.
   Examine whether the improvement process reproduces the same trap.
3. Ask how phenomena can be modeled in RxJS: psychology, a movie scene, a human
   exchange, software or an organization. Compare several perspectives and
   scales. Recurrent structures may reflect the phenomenon or the chosen
   vocabulary; preserve alternative models and failed predictions.
4. Explore variations of a pattern: reversal, slower or faster timing, exchanged
   roles, layering and small changes. The operator's GEB/Bach example motivates
   this exercise. Retain the original, the transformation, preserved properties
   and observed differences. It is not a request to add those operations to the
   closed executable enum in this order.
5. Treat metaphor, analogy, simile, anthropomorphic scenes and simulation as
   ways to make difficult ideas understandable and manipulable. Keep the idea,
   representation, experiment and cross-context correspondences distinguishable.
   A soldier scene need not determine the eventual application domain.

## Authoring layer and repository ownership

The intended beneficiaries include UIFA engineers who hand-craft actor behavior
from existing pieces or custom compositions, and testers who examine the
behavior in relevant circumstances. The operator expects some discoveries and
configurations to return to DotLn as primary skills, supports, interactions or
other reusable compositions.

The operator clarified that the likely destination is this higher-level
authoring layer, which may compile to a common IR. The IR compatibility document
is related, but it does not imply that every idea should be authored as IR or
that every successful experiment requires a new kernel construct. Some findings
may improve existing compositions.
